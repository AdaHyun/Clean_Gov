# What can community health workers do?

Identify links between health outcomes and sources of environmental and occupational exposure in children

Disseminate knowledge about the most common environmental risks and measures to prevent them

Identify and report to the local authorities the potential sources of environmental exposure

Be on the alert for cases of disease linked to environmental exposure

![](http://100.100.33.62:30900/wdzszt-docparse-test/126e10e7e21195d0895fed40bf56f4cc4307f8cd79e456f24471974dd6848b99.jpg)

Advocate, raise health concerns, influence decision- makers in implementing action to protect communities from exposure to environmental pollution

# Air pollution

# Health effects

Air pollution can result in:

reduced lung function and rate of lung growth, respiratory infections (for example, pneumonia), and exacerbation or aggravation of asthma;

pre-term birth, low birth weight, still birth and small for gestational age;

impairment of children’s cognitive and neurological development;

increased risk and incidence of otitis media;

development of some childhood cancers, including leukaemia;

# Air pollution

# Recommended actions

Be informed about the health effects of exposure to air pollution, especially on children

Advise families on ways of promoting sustainable lifestyles

Engage with other sectors and advocate adoption of air pollution policies at local and national levels

![](http://100.100.33.62:30900/wdzszt-docparse-test/ed00764ca6a23c6cbbf33c3221912f3592895cf185123adcebc4c97627ac3c72.jpg)

# Health effects

Exposure to lead in utero can cause reduced IQ, increased antisocial behaviour and reduced educational attainment

Exposure to mercury in utero can cause neurological and behavioural disorders

Acute pesticide poisoning is also a serious public health problem and children are particularly vulnerable to its effects

Many POPs and EDCs may impact the immune, reproductive and neurodevelopmental systems

Chemicals: heavy metals, persistent organic pollutants (POPs), pesticides and endocrine disrupting chemicals (EDCs)

# Recommended actions

Advise people to limit their intake of POPs and mercury from food by following the advice of the local food-safety authorities

Advise people against keeping toxic chemicals in the home. Promote the safe packaging and clear labelling of toxic substances, as well as their storage out of the reach of children to prevent poisoning

Identify and report to the local authorities the potential sources of environmental exposure, such as hazardous waste sites and electronic waste- recycling activities.

Advise parents and caretakers to prevent children from accessing waste sites

# Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH)

# Health effects

Lack of WASH is a major risk factor for diarrhoea, parasitic infections and malnutrition in children. Diarrhoea is the fourth leading cause of death among children under 5 years

Infections associated with unclean births account for 26% of neonatal deaths

Inadequate WASH in health-care facilities and the unsafe disposal of wastewater from health centres can contribute to the spread of antimicrobial resistance among patients and communities

The unsafe management of health-care waste exposes health-care workers, waste handlers, patients and their families, and the community to preventable infections and the effects of toxic substances, including injuries

# Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH)

# Recommended actions

Promote breastfeeding

Raise awareness about WASH-related practices

Contribute to health surveillance on WASH-related diseases with the aim of informing appropriate WASH-related actions
